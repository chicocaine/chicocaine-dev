# TITLE: The Cost of Efficiency

I started learning how to program right as I entered my first year at university as a computer science student. I would not say that I was good at programming. I could deal with simple code: variables, conditionals, loops, functions, and the like. However, it would take me a long time to know what a piece of code does. I even struggled with concepts most would consider basic programming paradigms.

One of my first software projects for a class was with a group. I tried my hand at writing some good code; well, I thought it was decent enough, only to be entirely rewritten by a peer better than I was. I ended up being *“the guy who buys snacks and coffee for the team”* or *“the guy who talks”*. Needless to say, I felt entirely [useless]{.danger}.

LLMs at this time are relatively on the young side. OpenAI just released ChatGPT about half a year ago, but I did not know anything about them until a quarter into 2023. Stumbling upon LLMs was like stumbling upon a list of GTA cheat codes. A hack. I could instruct it to get information or do things for me, and bam, just like that, seconds later we get some text. Was the output good? I wouldn’t know at that time. I was clueless that I couldn’t tell. For a beginner however, it was [empowering]{.success}.

Since then, when learning how to code, or when doing a project, the process almost always included an LLM in the middle. I mean, it was just more [efficient]{.primary} that way: I could instruct it to explain how snippets of code worked; I could instruct it to teach me how to code; I could instruct it to tell me what the best architecture is for a specific set of requirements; I could instruct it to write code for me; Fuck it, I could instruct it to tell me how to instruct it better. It felt so [fast]{.primary} and so [good]{.primary}. I could make decent projects. I can finally be [useful]{.success}.

A few more dances and now I’m tired. The way I use the *“tool”* feels like [slop]{.danger}. Projects became unsatisfyingly exhausting. I find it harder to be proud of what *“I”* made. I find myself asking instead of looking for answers. I find myself telling more than doing. I find myself learning for the sake of necessity, instead of curiosity. The efficiency that felt so good then now feels like [shit]{.danger}.

This is where I am currently at, and I would like to change. I’m looking for the joy of making cool shit. The passion that I had is probably still in there. I just need to slow down.

*P.S. I am no pro. Just a guy learning about stuff.*
